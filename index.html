First Principles

If you've never read a Chemours filing, start here

Chemours sells three categories of fluorine- and titanium-based chemistry that almost nobody outside the industry can name. Below: what the three segments actually make, who buys it, and why the company is interesting and dangerous at once.

⚪
What is TiO2 and why does it matter?
Titanium dioxide is the white pigment in roughly every white surface around you — paints, plastics, paper, sunscreen, food coloring. It scatters light with unmatched efficiency, giving whiteness, brightness and UV protection. Demand tracks construction, housing, and packaging — i.e. global GDP. Chemours' Ti-Pure brand is the largest TiO2 brand globally at ~11% of ~10M tons of nameplate capacity.
❄️
What is an HFO refrigerant?
HFO = hydrofluoroolefin. The next-generation refrigerant. The old HFCs (Freon R-134a etc.) have high Global Warming Potential — kilo-for-kilo they trap thousands of times more heat than CO2. HFOs contain a carbon double-bond that breaks down in days in the atmosphere, giving near-zero GWP. Regulators are forcibly switching every air conditioner, fridge and chiller from HFC to HFO. Chemours' Opteon is one of only two scale HFO franchises globally (Honeywell Solstice is the other).
🔬
What is a fluoropolymer?
Plastics built around the carbon-fluorine bond — the strongest single bond in organic chemistry. Result: extreme heat resistance, chemical inertness, lowest friction known. Used wherever ordinary plastics fail: semiconductor wet-chemistry tubing, EV battery binders (PVDF), aerospace seals, chemical reactors, data-center immersion-cooling baths. Chemours owns the Teflon, Viton, Krytox and Nafion brands — the original DuPont fluorochemistry portfolio.
⚠️
What are PFAS, and why is everyone suing?
Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — thousands of synthetic compounds defined by that same C-F bond. They don't break down in the environment — hence "forever chemicals". DuPont's PFOA (used to make Teflon) and 3M's PFOS (in firefighting foam) are tied to cancer cluster lawsuits. Chemours inherited every legacy PFAS site when it was spun out of DuPont in 2015. ~120-130k AFFF MDL personal-injury claims, >$750M of pending exposure outside the NJ settlement.
🧪
What is Nafion?
A proprietary ion-exchange membrane — sulfonated tetrafluoroethylene. The plastic equivalent of a salt bridge. Conducts protons, blocks gas. Used in: PEM hydrogen electrolyzers (splits water into H2 + O2), PEM fuel cells (the reverse — H2 → electricity), and chlor-alkali plants (chlorine production). The "green hydrogen optionality" everyone trades. Chemours just paused its France Nafion expansion — third-party demand projections came in too weak.
💧
What is two-phase immersion cooling?
AI servers run at 1 megawatt per rack — air cooling can't dump that much heat. Solution: submerge the servers in a fluorochemical bath that boils at ~50°C. The vapor condenses at the top of the tank and drips back down — passive heat removal. Uses ~60% less energy and 90% less water than chillers. Chemours' Opteon 2P50 was just qualified by Samsung for Gen-4 SSDs — first 2-phase fluid Samsung has ever approved. RBC sees $3B TAM producing EBITDA equal to entire Opteon franchise by 2030.
📜
What's the AIM Act?
U.S. American Innovation & Manufacturing Act, signed 2020. Mandates an 85% phase-down of HFCs by 2036 using a step-down quota system, with the EPA controlling quota allocation. Mirrors the global Kigali Amendment (2016) and the EU's revised F-Gas Regulation (full ban on high-GWP gases by 2030). This is the forced-conversion thesis behind every Opteon dollar of revenue. Bears: patents expire ~2030, generics arrive. Bulls: aftermarket installed base grows for decades regardless.
⚖️
What's the DuPont indemnity?
When DuPont spun off Chemours in 2015, it tried to hand over all the PFAS liability as well. After a 2017 restructure (and a near-default by Chemours), DuPont, Corteva (the spun-off ag business) and Chemours signed a $4 billion cost-sharing cap — Chemours pays 50%, DuPont 49%, Corteva 17% (overage covered). The trade today is essentially: how much $4B headroom is left vs. how fast the AFFF MDL eats it.
The investment debate in one line:  Is the Opteon ramp into 2036 + the 2-PIC data-center optionality + the chloride-route TiO2 cost advantage enough to outweigh ~$1.5B of PFAS net-present-value liability, a 4.9x leveraged balance sheet, and HFO patent expiry around 2030? Sell-side price targets span $12 (CFRA Sell) to $30 (UBS/Mizuho Buy) — a 2.5x range that captures exactly that tension.

Regulatory Cycle · Thermal & Specialized Solutions

The HFC-to-HFO forced switch

Every car AC unit, supermarket refrigeration case, and chiller in the U.S. has to swap its refrigerant by 2036 — by federal law. This is a forced replacement cycle, not a discretionary one. Chemours' Opteon franchise sits on one side of the trade; Honeywell's Solstice on the other. The only question for investors is what happens when the patents expire.

The Tail Risk

PFAS — how to underwrite "forever chemicals"

PFAS is the load-bearing bear case. Every Chemours fluoropolymer was historically made using PFAS processing aids (PFOA, then GenX). The legal architecture is a multi-front war: federal AFFF MDL in South Carolina, state AG suits, and the DuPont/Corteva indemnity MOU that ring-fences part of it. Understanding the $4B cap and where Chemours sits inside it is the single biggest variant view on the stock.

PFAS in one slide
PFAS = per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances. Thousands of compounds, all defined by at least one fully-fluorinated carbon. The C-F bond is so strong they don't break down in nature — "forever chemicals". They bio-accumulate in blood and tissue.

Long-chain: PFOA, PFOS — original DuPont/3M chemistry. Linked in epidemiology studies to kidney/testicular cancer, ulcerative colitis, thyroid disease, pregnancy-induced hypertension. Phased out post-2015.

Short-chain (next-gen): GenX (HFPO-DA) — Chemours' own replacement chemistry, developed in 2009 as a polymerization aid. Marketed as safer. Regulators don't fully buy it — EPA set a 10 ppt drinking water limit (vs 4 ppt for PFOA). EU REACH proposes banning PFAS as a class, including short-chain.
The $4B cost-sharing cap — what's been spent, what's left
The trade: AFFF MDL personal-injury claims at 120-130k with 15,000+ active lawsuits. Bellwether trials late 2026-2027. If the next round of settlements consumes the remaining $1.2-1.4B headroom, Chemours "would suddenly assume full, unmitigated exposure" (Capstone DC).

Governance reset still recent 2024 CFO removal

Nafion / hydrogen is a story, not a number long-dated
Chemours just placed Villers-Saint-Paul Nafion capacity expansion on long-term hold — third-party demand projections for hydrogen came in below expectations. The hydrogen optionality is genuinely real but is a 2030+ event, not a 2026-2028 earnings driver.
